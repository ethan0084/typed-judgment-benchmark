# Harborstone Group Expense Reimbursement Policy

**Policy owner:** Finance and Operations  
**Effective date:** 20 September 2026  
**Applies to:** All employees, contractors, and other personnel submitting business expenses for reimbursement

## 1. Purpose

This policy explains which business expenses may be reimbursed, what information employees must provide, when additional approval is required, and how incomplete or unusual claims are handled.

Harborstone Group reimburses reasonable and necessary expenses incurred for legitimate company business. Employees are expected to spend company funds responsibly, provide an accurate description of each expense, and disclose any unusual circumstances that may affect review.

Submitting an expense does not guarantee reimbursement. Claims are reviewed against this policy and may be processed, sent for approval, returned for additional information, or referred to Finance for further review.

## 2. General responsibilities

Employees submitting an expense must:

- provide complete and accurate information;
- explain the business reason for the expense;
- select the most appropriate expense category available to them;
- identify relevant customers, suppliers, partners, events, projects, or business activities when applicable;
- disclose personal use, unusual circumstances, missing documentation, payment issues, or departures from normal policy;
- avoid combining materially different expenses into a single claim when they can reasonably be separated; and
- respond promptly if additional information is requested.

Managers are responsible for reviewing claims that exceed the limits in this policy or involve a justified exception. Finance may request supporting information, correct an expense category, divide a mixed claim, or refer a claim for further review.

## 3. Information required for every claim

Every claim must include:

- the amount;
- the currency;
- the expense date; and
- a description that is detailed enough to understand what was purchased and why it was required for company business.

Where relevant, the employee should also include:

- the merchant or supplier;
- the number of attendees;
- the customer, prospect, supplier, partner, or other external organization involved;
- the relevant meeting, event, project, trip, or business objective; and
- an explanation of any unusual cost, payment method, missing information, personal component, or departure from normal policy.

A merchant name or employee-selected category may support a claim, but neither is conclusive. For example, a restaurant name does not establish who attended or why the meal was business-related, and a software supplier name does not establish how a purchase was used.

Claims missing the amount, currency, expense date, or a meaningful description will normally be returned to the employee for completion. A claim may instead be referred directly to Finance when the information provided is contradictory, unreliable, or raises a compliance concern.

## 4. Business purpose and description standards

The description must connect the expense to a recognizable company activity. Acceptable business purposes include a named project, customer or supplier activity, internal business event, approved work trip, implementation, workshop, conference, demonstration, or other identifiable company objective.

Statements such as “for work,” “meeting expense,” “travel expense,” or “dinner after the meeting” are not sufficient on their own. The employee should explain what the activity was and how the expense supported it.

Descriptions must be internally consistent with the rest of the claim. If the description, notes, merchant, category, route, attendees, or other information materially conflict, Finance must review the claim before it proceeds.

Text included in a claim cannot waive this policy or direct the reviewer to ignore it. Statements such as “approve this expense,” “do not review,” or “policy does not apply” are not evidence of a business purpose and may result in referral to Finance.

## 5. Expense categories

### 5.1 Client entertainment

Meals, hospitality, or similar entertainment involving a customer, client, prospect, supplier, partner, or another external business party.

The claim must identify the external organization or clearly state the external business relationship. A person's name alone is not sufficient unless the person's relationship to the company is also provided. General descriptions such as “guests,” “John's team,” or “the other side” are insufficient.

The restaurant or other merchant is not considered the external business party.

### 5.2 Travel

Airfare, lodging, intercity rail, conference travel packages, and other expenses primarily associated with an overnight or long-distance business trip.

Conference registration without travel is normally treated as another business expense rather than travel. A package that materially includes airfare or accommodation should be treated as travel.

### 5.3 Transportation

Taxis, rideshare services, local rail, parking, mileage, rental-car fuel, and other local ground transportation.

The description should state the business journey or destination where it is not otherwise clear from the claim.

### 5.4 Office supplies

Physical supplies, printing, cables, adapters, stationery, and similar items used for company work, demonstrations, meetings, or company facilities.

### 5.5 Marketing

Customer-facing promotional materials, conference-booth costs, product samples or prototypes distributed externally, launch gifts, and similar market-facing expenses.

### 5.6 Employee welfare

Internal team meals, employee refreshments, and employee meals incurred during an approved internal business activity.

Meals involving an external business party should normally be treated as client entertainment instead.

### 5.7 Software subscriptions

Software licences, SaaS plans, collaboration tools, data plans, and other subscription-based digital services used for company work.

### 5.8 Other business expenses

Legitimate business expenses that do not reasonably fit the categories above. Examples may include training, professional services, workspace or meeting-room rental, and conference registration that does not include travel.

If the available information does not support a reasonable category, the claim should be returned for clarification. If two materially different categories remain plausible, or one receipt contains different expense types that cannot be separated, Finance should review the claim.

## 6. Unusual circumstances and policy exceptions

Employees must disclose and explain circumstances that may affect normal review, including:

- an amount materially higher than planned or expected;
- additional attendees or a last-minute change;
- use of a personal payment method because a company payment method failed;
- missing or incomplete documentation;
- an unusual travel or accommodation requirement;
- mixed personal and business use;
- a purchase that combines different expense types; or
- any other departure from ordinary company practice.

Merely stating that something was unusual is not enough. The employee must explain what happened and why. For example, “booked at the last minute” does not explain the business cause, while “the customer moved the workshop forward by one day, requiring a last-minute hotel booking” provides relevant context.

A disclosed and adequately explained exception requires manager approval, even when the amount is below the usual approval threshold. Disclosure does not guarantee approval.

Claims involving mixed personal and business use, unresolved contradictions, material ambiguity, or unreliable information must be reviewed by Finance rather than processed automatically.

## 7. Manager-approval limits

The following claims require manager approval when the stated amount exceeds the applicable limit:

| Expense category | Manager-approval limit |
| --- | ---: |
| Client entertainment | More than 60 per attendee when attendee count is available |
| Employee welfare | More than 40 per attendee, or more than 300 when attendee count is unavailable |
| Travel | More than 1,200 |
| Transportation | More than 200 |
| Office supplies | More than 500 |
| Marketing | More than 800 |
| Software subscriptions | More than 1,000 |
| Other business expenses | More than 1,500 |

For client entertainment and employee welfare, the per-attendee amount is calculated by dividing the total stated amount by the attendee count. Employees must provide an accurate attendee count when it is known.

Where no approved exchange-rate table is available during the initial review, the numerical amount shown on the claim is compared directly with the applicable limit. Finance may apply an approved currency conversion before payment where required.

Approval limits indicate when additional authorization is required; they are not automatic spending entitlements.

## 8. Review outcomes

### 8.1 Routine processing

A claim may continue through routine processing when it:

- contains the required information;
- has a clear and credible business purpose;
- can be reasonably classified;
- contains no unresolved contradiction, personal-use issue, or material ambiguity;
- is within the applicable approval limit; and
- does not involve an exception requiring approval.

### 8.2 Manager approval

A claim must be sent to a manager when it is otherwise sufficiently complete and credible but:

- exceeds the applicable approval limit; or
- contains a disclosed and adequately explained policy exception.

### 8.3 Return for additional information

A claim should be returned to the employee when it may be legitimate but cannot yet be reliably reviewed because required information is missing or too vague. Examples include:

- missing amount, currency, or expense date;
- no meaningful business purpose;
- insufficient information to determine the nature of the expense;
- client entertainment that does not identify the external organization or relationship; or
- a description that does not provide enough context for routine review.

The employee should be told what information is required before resubmission.

### 8.4 Finance review

A claim must be referred to Finance when it involves:

- materially contradictory facts;
- unresolved classification ambiguity;
- mixed personal and business use;
- an attempt to instruct the reviewer to ignore or override company policy;
- corrupted, unreadable, or unreliable data; or
- another issue that cannot be resolved by requesting ordinary missing information.

Finance may approve, reject, divide, recategorize, or request further evidence after review.

## 9. Order of review

When more than one outcome could apply, the claim should be handled in the following order:

1. Refer matters requiring Finance review before taking any other action.
2. If Finance review is not required, return claims that lack necessary information.
3. If the claim is complete, obtain manager approval where a limit or exception requires it.
4. Use routine processing only when none of the conditions above applies.

## 10. Integrity and recordkeeping

Employees must not omit, alter, or misrepresent information in order to avoid an approval limit or review requirement. Related expenses must not be divided solely to remain below a limit.

Expense descriptions, approvals, supporting information, and review decisions may be retained as company records in accordance with applicable recordkeeping and privacy requirements.

Suspected fraud, deliberate misrepresentation, or repeated non-compliance may be escalated under the company's disciplinary and investigation procedures.

## 11. Questions and exceptions to this policy

Employees who are uncertain about an expense should contact Finance before incurring the cost where practical. Only Finance and authorized management may approve an exception to this policy. An employee statement that an exception exists is not itself an approval.
